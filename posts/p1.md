The **BlaBla Corp** brief asks us to build something that feels genuinely tailored to a specific community — not just a generic social feed. Reading it, I kept thinking: **what community do I actually understand well enough to design for?**

As someone who loves **arts and crafts** I thought about people who have similar interests — people who do things like jigsaw puzzles, bead art (hama beads, perler beads), embroidery, or other slow, tactile hobbies. It's a niche that's grown a lot online but doesn't have a dedicated platform that takes it seriously.

The feature I'm most interested in exploring is a project log. Craft projects take time — sometimes weeks. Having a place to document progress, note what materials you used, and mark something as "finished" feels genuinely **useful and satisfying**. It's different from just posting a photo because the data is structured: project name, type, difficulty, start date, completion date, notes.

Functional requirements I'm starting with:
- Users can **create a project entry with title, craft type, and status (in progress / completed)**
- Users can add progress notes over time
- Completed **projects are displayed in a gallery-style view** for the community to browse

What I like about this concept is that the data model is relatively clean. A project has a one-to-many relationship with progress notes — that's a straightforward SQL structure and feels achievable within the constraints of this assignment.

What I'm less sure about is the visual side. Craft communities are very image-driven, but the brief doesn't require image uploads as a core feature. I'll need to think about how to make the interface feel engaging even without photos — maybe through tags, categories, or some kind of community stats.

Still early days, but I think this direction has potential.
